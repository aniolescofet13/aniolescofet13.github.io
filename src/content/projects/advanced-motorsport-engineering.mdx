A laboratory course at Politecnico di Milano using lap time simulation as the central engineering thread, supported by tyre, braking, and data acquisition labs. The basics session covers constant-radius cornering, minimum-time optimization on an oval, coast-down parameter identification, vertical loads under braking, and aerodynamic estimation from GPS data. Two progressive lap time simulation labs build from a quasi-steady-state framework with ride height and lift-and-coast sensitivity studies up to an advanced optimal control formulation using Direct Legendre Collocation, generating optimal racing lines, speed profiles, driver inputs, and tyre friction utilization maps. Supporting labs characterize tyre thermal behavior and its effect on lap time through toe angle, weight distribution, and aero balance sweeps, and identify the friction law from experimental data. A braking lab covers disc thermal analysis, braking system sizing, and bicycle model validation. A hands-on session with Marelli Wintax addresses real-car data acquisition and telemetry processing, and industry seminars from Scuderia Ferrari, Pirelli, and Ducati Corse round out the course with applied perspectives on F1 performance engineering, racing tyre design, and motorcycle lap time simulation.
